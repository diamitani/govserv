# New Mexico — GovServ services

31 verified services, verified 2026-09-19. Sources are official pages fetched and read before inclusion.

## Metro (city services)

### Albuquerque

- **ABQ RIDE — Albuquerque Bus Service** (Transportation) — Albuquerque's main public bus system: fixed routes plus ART rapid transit run on set routes across the city. All ABQ RIDE services are Zero Fares — ride for free, no pass or reservation needed.
  City of Albuquerque Transit Department (ABQ RIDE). Cost: Free — Zero Fares program.
  243-RIDE (7433) · [ABQ RIDE routes and schedules (City of Albuquerque)](https://www.cabq.gov/transit/routes-and-schedules/routes-and-schedules)
- **ABQ311 — Albuquerque Community Contact Center** (Legal Aid) — One-stop contact for all non-emergency City of Albuquerque services and questions: report potholes, graffiti, weeds, missed trash pickup, broken streetlights, and abandoned vehicles, or request services like large-item garbage pickup and new trash carts. Reach ABQ311 online or through the channels listed on the ABQ311 page.
  City of Albuquerque (311 Division). Cost: Free.
  [ABQ311 Community Contact Center (City of Albuquerque)](https://www.cabq.gov/311)
- **Public Library — Albuquerque/Bernalillo County** (Education) — 19 public library branches across Albuquerque and Bernalillo County with free Wi-Fi at every location, books, eBooks, eResources, events, and services like Tech Turn Up (free tech help, no appointment needed at Central & Unser) and ESL conversation groups. Library cards are free for New Mexico residents; apply in person at any branch with photo ID and proof of mailing address — your card works right away at all branches and for all eResources.
  City of Albuquerque (Public Library Albuquerque and Bernalillo County). Cost: Free.
  505-768-5141; 505-768-4320 · [Get a library card (abqlibrary.org)](https://abqlibrary.org/librarycards)

### Las Cruces

- **Las Cruces Public Libraries** (Education) — Las Cruces' public library system: Thomas Branigan Memorial Library (200 E Picacho Ave, Mon–Thu 9 a.m.–8 p.m., Fri–Sat 9 a.m.–6 p.m.) and the Munson Branch (975 S Mesquite St, Mon–Fri 10 a.m.–2:30 p.m.). Books, digital resources, computer labs, and programs for all ages.
  City of Las Cruces. Cost: Free.
  575-528-4000; 575-528-3406 · [Las Cruces Public Libraries (lascruces.gov)](https://lascruces.gov/arts-and-leisure/libraries/)
- **Roadrunner Transit — Las Cruces Bus Service** (Transportation) — Las Cruces' public bus system: 8 fixed routes serving shopping areas, employment centers, healthcare facilities, NMSU, and recreation destinations. All bus rides are zero-fare.
  City of Las Cruces. Cost: Free — zero-fare service.
  [Roadrunner Transit routes and info (City of Las Cruces)](https://lascruces.gov/community/transportation/roadrunner-transit/)

### Rio Rancho

- **Rio Metro — Rio Rancho Demand-Response Transit** (Transportation) — Public door-to-door shared-ride service within Rio Rancho city limits, plus fixed bus routes connecting Rio Rancho to the Rail Runner and Albuquerque. Rio Rancho demand-response is for seniors 62+ and people with disabilities 18+ (Valencia County service is open to the general public). Reserve by phone one day in advance before noon. Fares apply — check the Rider's Guide (dated September 2022; confirm current fares when booking).
  Rio Metro Regional Transit District. Cost: Fares apply; confirm current fares when booking (published Rider's Guide is from 2022).
  505-404-2063; 1-866-795-7245 · [Rio Metro Rider's Guide (policies, fares, how to schedule)](https://riometro.org/DocumentCenter/View/472/Rio-Metro-Riders-Guide---English-PDF?bidId=)
- **Rio Rancho Public Library** (Education) — Rio Rancho's public library system: Loma Colorado Main Library, Esther Bone Memorial Library, and The HUB @ Enchanted Hills (Tue–Sat 9 a.m.–6 p.m.). Books, ebooks, movies, Wi-Fi, public computers, checkout kits, printing, and meeting rooms. One Rio Rancho library card works at all three locations. Call 505-891-5008 for library information.
  City of Rio Rancho. Cost: Free.
  505-891-5008 · [Rio Rancho library information (rrnm.gov)](https://rrnm.gov/4838/The-HUB)

### Roswell

- **Roswell Transit — Fixed Route & Diala-Ride** (Transportation) — Roswell's public bus system: daily fixed-route service from the Roswell Air Center/ENMU to the Roswell Mall, Diala-Ride on-demand service to stops off the fixed route (call 575-624-6766, vehicle usually arrives in under 20 minutes), federally mandated ADA complementary paratransit, and airport shuttle service. Fares: $0.25 one-way for all riders (children under 5, veterans, and personal attendants ride free); paratransit punch cards 20 rides for $6.00.
  City of Roswell. Cost: $0.25 one-way; free for children under 5, military veterans, and personal attendants.
  (575) 624-6766 · [Roswell Transit Passenger Guide & Schedule (roswell-nm.gov)](https://Roswell-nm.gov/DocumentCenter/View/25723/2024-Transit-Guide)

### Santa Fe

- **Santa Fe Public Library** (Education) — Public library system for Santa Fe with multiple branches offering books, media, museum passes, technology (hotspots, laptops), no-fine overdue policy (blocking only after 28 days), and automatic renewals. Register in person at the Circulation desk with picture ID and proof of mailing address. Anyone who lives, works, or goes to school in the City of Santa Fe or Santa Fe County qualifies; holders of any other New Mexico public library card are also eligible.
  City of Santa Fe. Cost: Free.
  [Get a library card (santafelibrary.org)](https://www.santafelibrary.org/get-a-card)
- **Santa Fe Trails — City Bus Service** (Transportation) — Santa Fe's public bus system with fixed routes running daily, 7 a.m.–8 p.m. Fares: $1 one-way adult, $0.50 half fare (seniors 60+, people with qualifying disabilities, students), free for youth 18 and younger, active-duty military, veterans, and city employees.
  City of Santa Fe Transit Division. Cost: $1 one-way adult; $0.50 half fare; free for youth 18 and under, veterans, active-duty military, city employees.
  505-955-2001; 866-551-7433 · [Santa Fe Transit Division — fares, routes, schedules (City of Santa Fe)](https://santafenm.gov/public-works/transit)

## Statewide

- **Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC)** (Seniors) — Statewide information and assistance hub for older New Mexicans, people with disabilities, and caregivers. The ADRC connects callers to home and community-based services, in-home services, health-insurance counseling, and caregiver and nutrition supports. Call Monday–Friday, 7:45 a.m.–5 p.m. MT.
  New Mexico Aging and Long-Term Services Department (ALTSD). Cost: Free.
  1-800-432-2080 · [ALTSD Contact Us — ADRC, ombudsman, office info (aging.nm.gov)](https://www.aging.nm.gov/contact-us/)
- **Division of Vocational Rehabilitation (DVR)** (Disability) — Free employment services helping New Mexicans with disabilities (ages 14+) secure and retain work matching their abilities and interests. A comprehensive assessment determines goals and services — vocational rehabilitation counseling, career planning, ADA/accommodation guidance, assistive technology, job training, and Social Security Ticket to Work information. Offices in Albuquerque, Las Cruces, Rio Rancho, Roswell, Santa Fe, and more.
  New Mexico Division of Vocational Rehabilitation. Cost: Free.
  (800) 224-7005; (505) 954-8500; (575) 524-6135; (505) 896-4500; (575) 624-6024; (505) 383-2500 · [NM DVR locations and info (sharenm.org)](https://sharenm.org/nm-division-of-vocational-rehabilitation-dvr/new-mexico-dept-of-vocational-rehabilitation)
- **Driver Licenses and ID Cards — NM Motor Vehicle Division** (Transportation) — Issues REAL ID and Standard driver licenses and identification cards at MVD field offices statewide. First-time applicants bring 1 proof of ID number, 1 proof of identity, and 2 proofs of NM residency; eye, written, and road tests are required for first-time licensees. New residents must surrender their out-of-state license and apply for a NM license once residency is established. Fees are $18 for a 4-year license or $34 for an 8-year license.
  New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division (MVD), Taxation & Revenue Department. Cost: $18 for a 4-year license; $34 for an 8-year license.
  [Apply for a new driving credential (MVD)](https://www.mvd.newmexico.gov/nm-drivers-licenses-ids/drivers-license/apply-for-a-new-driving-credential/)
- **ECECD Home Visiting** (Family & Childcare) — Free in-home visits from a trained expert for New Mexico families with children ages prenatal to five — regardless of income. Visits cover safe sleep, injury prevention, nutrition, breastfeeding, child care guidance, and parenting support.
  New Mexico Early Childhood Education & Care Department (ECECD). Cost: Free.
  [ECECD family supports — includes Home Visiting (nmececd.org)](https://www.nmececd.org/2023/08/09/ececd-offers-expansive-supports-to-families-as-children-head-back-to-school/)
- **Families FIRST Perinatal Case Management** (Family & Childcare) — Free support during pregnancy, infancy, and the first three years of a child's life for Medicaid-eligible New Mexico families. Families FIRST nurses provide education, resources, and advice on pregnancy, health, nutrition, and nurturing to help families get a healthy start.
  New Mexico Early Childhood Education & Care Department (ECECD). Cost: Free.
  1-877-842-4152 · [ECECD family supports — includes Families FIRST (nmececd.org)](https://www.nmececd.org/2023/08/09/ececd-offers-expansive-supports-to-families-as-children-head-back-to-school/)
- **Family Infant Toddler (FIT) Early Intervention** (Disability) — Free evaluations and early intervention services for New Mexico infants and toddlers with suspected developmental delays or disabilities. If you are concerned your baby or toddler is not developing as expected, FIT provides therapies and developmental services and coaches families on supporting development at home — free for all families.
  New Mexico Early Childhood Education & Care Department (ECECD). Cost: Free — evaluations and services are free for all families.
  [ECECD family supports — includes FIT early intervention (nmececd.org)](https://www.nmececd.org/2023/08/09/ececd-offers-expansive-supports-to-families-as-children-head-back-to-school/)
- **Farmers' Market Nutrition Program** (Seniors) — Seasonal benefits (May–October each year) to buy fresh fruits and vegetables at approved farmers' markets. Senior FMNP: NM residents age 60+ (or Native American age 55+) with qualifying income apply online or at senior centers, CSFP sites, public-health WIC clinics, or the state office. WIC FMNP: active WIC participants who are pregnant, breastfeeding, postpartum, or infants/children 6 months to 5 years apply through their local WIC clinic.
  New Mexico Department of Health (DOH) WIC. Cost: Free benefit.
  505-469-0548; 505-487-0904; doh.fmnp@doh.nm.gov · [NM Farmers' Market Nutrition Program (nmwic.org)](https://www.nmwic.org/fmnp/)
- **LIHEAP — Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program** (Utilities) — Helps eligible lower-income households with heating and cooling costs. Crisis LIHEAP moves faster for households with disconnected service, a disconnect notice, or nearly depleted bulk fuel (wood, propane). Expect an interview about ten days after applying and a decision letter within 45 days. HCA also contracts with the NM Mortgage Finance Authority for weatherization (weather stripping, windows, furnaces, insulation).
  New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA), Income Support Division. Cost: Free.
  1-800-283-4465 · [LIHEAP program info and what to bring (HCA)](https://www.hsd.state.nm.us/lookingforassistance/low_income_home_energy_assistance_program/)
- **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** (Seniors) — Free, confidential, statewide advocacy for residents of nursing homes, assisted living, and other long-term care facilities. Ombudsmen advocate for residents' rights, investigate complaints, help resolve concerns, and work to ensure quality of care.
  New Mexico Aging and Long-Term Services Department (ALTSD). Cost: Free — confidential statewide.
  1-866-451-2901 · [ALTSD Contact Us — ombudsman hotline (aging.nm.gov)](https://www.aging.nm.gov/contact-us/)
- **NM Opportunity & Lottery Scholarships — Tuition-Free College** (Education) — New Mexico's tuition-free college program: the Lottery Scholarship covers recent high school graduates enrolling full time, and the Opportunity Scholarship covers returning adult learners, part-time students, and students pursuing high-demand career training certificates. Most NM students qualify and can have required fees covered. There is no separate application — the 29 partnering public colleges and universities award the funds directly.
  New Mexico Higher Education Department (HED). Cost: Free.
  1-800-279-9777; fin.aid@hed.nm.gov · [Tuition-free college Q&A (NM Higher Education Department)](https://hed.nm.gov/news/new-mexico-higher-education-department-addresses-questions-on-tuition-free-college)
- **New Mexico Legal Aid** (Legal Aid) — Free civil legal services statewide for low-income New Mexicans and for victims of violence. Help with public benefits, affordable housing and landlord/tenant issues, domestic violence and family law, foreclosure, consumer rights, migrant farmworkers, veterans, and low-income taxpayer disputes with the IRS. Services range from advice to full representation.
  New Mexico Legal Aid. Cost: Free.
  1-833-545-4357; 1-866-416-1922 · [New Mexico Legal Aid — call or apply online](https://newmexicolegalaid.org/?p=731)
- **New Mexico PreK** (Education) — Free preschool for three- and four-year-olds delivered in community-based, school-based, and some Head Start settings.
  New Mexico Early Childhood Education & Care Department (ECECD). Cost: Free.
  [ECECD family supports — includes New Mexico PreK (nmececd.org)](https://www.nmececd.org/2023/08/09/ececd-offers-expansive-supports-to-families-as-children-head-back-to-school/)
- **SNAP — Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program** (Food) — Monthly food benefits on an EBT card for eligible low-income households; eligibility depends on income, household size, and expenses. SNAP Outreach partners give free help with applications, recertifications, and document submission, and multilingual help is available.
  New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA). Cost: Free.
  1-800-283-4465; 844-684-6268; 505-531-5556; 505-787-4009 · [SNAP program info and how to apply (HCA)](https://www.hca.nm.gov/lookingforassistance/snap-outreach/)
- **SOS Business Services — Start & Maintain a Business** (Small Business) — New Mexico's one-stop shop for business registration: corporations, LLCs, and partnerships must register with the Secretary of State. All business filings have moved online — no paper filings accepted. File, report, amend, change your registered agent, or obtain certificates through the online filing portal. Walk-in hours Monday–Thursday; online and phone services also available.
  New Mexico Secretary of State. Cost: Free to browse; filing fees vary by form.
  505-827-3600 · [SOS Business Services (sos.nm.gov)](https://www.sos.nm.gov/business-services/)
- **TANF / NMWorks — Cash Assistance** (Money & Taxes) — Monthly cash assistance for families with dependent children to meet basic needs like housing, utilities, and clothing. In New Mexico the federal TANF program is known as NMWorks, administered by HCA's Income Support Division.
  New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA). Cost: Free.
  1-800-283-4465 · [TANF/NMWorks cash assistance overview (HCA)](https://www.hca.nm.gov/2023/09/01/state-announces-a-23-percent-increase-in-cash-assistance-for-low-income-new-mexico-families/)
- **Turquoise Care — New Mexico Medicaid** (Healthcare) — New Mexico's Medicaid managed care program (renamed Turquoise Care effective July 1, 2024). Members select among four managed care organizations — Blue Cross Blue Shield of New Mexico, Presbyterian Health Plan, Molina, and UnitedHealthcare — covering physical health, behavioral health, and long-term services. Most Medicaid members are in managed care; Native Americans may opt in.
  New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA). Cost: Free.
  1-800-283-4465; 1-888-997-2583 · [Turquoise Care overview and how to apply (HCA)](https://www.hca.nm.gov/turquoise-care/)
- **Unemployment Insurance — NMDWS** (Employment) — Temporary wage replacement for workers who lost their jobs through no fault of their own. File a new claim online through the NM Workforce Connection system or by phone; weekly certifications and at least two work searches per week are required unless exempted.
  New Mexico Department of Workforce Solutions (NMDWS). Cost: Free — there is never a fee to file.
  1-877-664-6984 · [How to file for unemployment in NM (Albuquerque Public Library guide, cites NMDWS)](https://libanswers.abqlibrary.org/branches/faq/27738)
- **Universal Child Care Assistance** (Family & Childcare) — Effective November 1, 2025, New Mexico offers no-cost universal child care for families that are working or in school — regardless of income, no copays, no fees. Grandparents raising grandchildren, families caring for substance-exposed babies, families experiencing housing instability, and CYFD-involved families do not need to meet the work/school requirement. Includes centers, home-based providers, before/after-school, and summer programs. Only the child's immigration status is considered.
  New Mexico Early Childhood Education & Care Department (ECECD). Cost: Free — no copays or fees for families.
  1-800-691-9067 · [Universal child care at-a-glance for families (ECECD)](https://www.nmececd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/At-A-Glance-for-Families-Sept-2025_FINAL_ENG_SPAN.pdf)
- **WIC — Women, Infants, and Children** (Food) — Monthly food benefits loaded to a WIC EBT card plus nutrition education, breastfeeding support, and health referrals for pregnant, postpartum, and breastfeeding women, infants, and children under 5, served through public health clinics statewide.
  New Mexico Department of Health (NMDOH), Public Health Division. Cost: Free.
  575-624-6171; 575-528-5047; 505-469-0548 · [Find a WIC clinic (official NM WIC)](https://www.nmwic.org/locations/)

## Federal

- **Medicare — Parts A & B** (Seniors) — Federal health insurance for people 65 and older and certain younger people with disabilities. Part A covers hospital insurance; Part B covers medical insurance (doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services). Sign up through Social Security: apply online (easiest and fastest), visit your local Social Security office, or have the employer fill out CMS-L564 when losing job-based coverage. About two weeks after signing up you get a welcome package with your Medicare card.
  Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Cost: See Medicare.gov for current premiums and cost-sharing.
  1-800-772-1213 · [Sign up for Medicare Parts A & B (medicare.gov)](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/ready-to-sign-up-for-part-a-part-b)
- **VA Health Care** (Veterans) — Comprehensive health coverage for eligible veterans: primary care, specialists, prescriptions, mental health, and more. Apply online, by phone, by mail (VA Form 10-10EZ to the Health Eligibility Center, PO Box 5207, Janesville, WI 53547-5207), in person at any VA medical center or clinic, or with an accredited VSO representative. VA typically decides in less than a week.
  U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). Cost: Free to apply; copays may apply depending on priority group.
  877-222-8387 · [How to apply for VA health care (VA)](https://www.va.gov/health-care/how-to-apply/)
